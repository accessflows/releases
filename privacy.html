AccessFlows is local-first: the responses you get back, your history and your secrets stay on your machine. This page says exactly what leaves it — and only when you sign in, share something, or turn on a feature that needs someone else's server.

Who is responsible for your data

AccessFlows Co., Ltd. — 36 Trịnh Đình Thảo, phường Tân Phú, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam — is the controller of what this policy describes.

Everything in this policy, including a request for your data: legal@accessflows.com.

1. What never leaves your computer

These are stored locally, in the application's own store, and are not uploaded anywhere:

  • the responses your requests come back with, and the history of what you sent;
  • values you mark as secret — in an environment, in the globals, in the application's secret store;
  • certificates and keys, and the files you keep inside a collection;
  • your preferences, and the API key you give an AI provider.

We do not run a proxy in the middle of your API traffic: requests go from your machine to the endpoint you named, and we never see them or the responses. The app also counts nothing: no analytics, no crash reports, no usage events — there is no telemetry in the product at all.

2. What is synchronised when you sign in

Signing in switches the app to your account's workspace, and keeps it in step with our servers — that is what makes the same workspace open on another machine. These are the records that are synchronised:

Record What it carries
Collections the name, description, headers, authentication settings and scripts
Requests the URL, method, headers, body and tests of each request in the collection — with the response left behind
Environments and globals the variables and their values, except any variable marked secret, which is filtered out before the record is queued

Responses, history, secret values and files are never part of this. A record that cannot be uploaded waits on your machine and is sent when the connection returns.

The workspace you have open is the one that synchronises; the local workspace (the app before you sign in) never does. Signing out stops it.

3. What you share, and for whom

A collection you share is uploaded so the people you shared it with can open it, together with the name and description you gave it. A private share is readable by the people you named, and a share to the Community is readable by anyone with the link and appears in the directory.

A collection whose environments carry secret values cannot be published until those values are removed — the app refuses, so a published collection never contains one.

Unsharing deletes the stored copy shortly after. A revoked business verification hides the Community shares of that account and deletes them 30 days later, unless it is restored first. Deleting your account removes everything of yours that our servers hold — see section 9 for what outlives it.

4. The account itself

An account needs an email address, and an account is only created if you choose to make one.

  • Email address — to sign you in, confirm the address, and reset a password.
  • Account identifier — a random id that ties your workspaces, shares, plan and settings to you.
  • Sign-in provider — whether the account signs in with a password, Google, GitHub or Microsoft. With a provider we receive the address and the identifier it gives us, never a password.
  • Profile you choose to set — a display name and an avatar, if you add them.
  • Which terms you accepted — the version, and when you accepted it. It is recorded once, at the first sign-in, so that an acceptance can be shown rather than assumed. An account created before 25 November 2026 — the day the notice began to appear at sign-in — has no such record, because it was never shown one; we would rather leave that field empty than fill it with a claim nobody made.
  • Plan and subscription — the plan, the seats, the store's identifiers for the subscription, and its state. Not your card.
  • A business verification, if you file one — the business details you typed, the documents you uploaded (business registration, domain ownership, anything additional you attach), the decision and its reason.
  • Support conversations — what you send us when you ask for help, which we keep for as long as the request needs.

Our servers also write ordinary logs while they serve a request: an IP address, a timestamp, the function called and whether it failed. They are kept by our host for days rather than months.

5. AI features

The AI assistant works with an API key you supply, for OpenAI, Anthropic or an OpenAI-compatible endpoint of your choosing. The key is stored on your machine, and the request carrying your question goes from your machine straight to that provider — not through us.

  • We never receive your prompts, the code sent with them, or the answers.
  • Before a question is sent, response bodies are reduced to their shape — keys and value types, with the values removed — so the model sees the structure of a response and not its contents.
  • What that provider does with the request is governed by your agreement with it. Read its policy; the choice of endpoint is yours and so is the data that reaches it.
  • Removing the key in preferences stops the feature; nothing of it is left on our side.

6. Payments

Payments are taken by Creem, which is the merchant of record. The card, the billing address and the invoice live in Creem's systems under its own privacy policy, and the tax it collects is handled there. We keep the subscription's identifier, its state, the plan and the seat count — and an archive of the identifiers of a subscription that has ended, so that a cancelled plan is not granted again by a late message from the store.

10. Security

Accounts and shares are guarded by row-level rules: a share is served only to the account that owns it and to the people it was shared with, a synchronised record only to the account it belongs to, and a verification document only to the desk reviewing it — from a bucket that is not public. Secrets are filtered out of synchronisation and publication rather than merely hidden.

No one here reads your synchronised workspace or your shares as a matter of course. We look at them only when you ask us to, when a report of abuse or infringement needs an answer, or when the law requires it.

Locally, the security of your data is the security of your machine: keep it patched, and keep the app's store out of folders you sync publicly. If a breach ever affects your data, we tell you without undue delay, and the authorities where the law requires it.

11. Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us to:

  • show you what we hold about you, and give you a copy in a portable form;
  • correct what is wrong — much of it is yours to edit in the app;
  • delete it, either from the app or by asking us;
  • restrict or object to a use that rests on our legitimate interest;
  • withdraw a consent you gave, without it affecting what happened before.

Write to legal@accessflows.com and we answer within 30 days. If you are not satisfied with the answer, you may complain to the data protection authority where you live — in Vietnam, the authority that oversees information security.

Nothing here gives you a right over data we never received: your responses, your history, your secrets and your AI key are on your machine, and deleting them is something only you can do.

12. Automated decisions

A business verification is decided by a person, not by software. Nothing else about your account is decided automatically in a way that has a legal or similarly significant effect on you.

13. Children

The application is a tool for building and testing APIs and is not directed at children. An account is for someone at least 13 years old, and 16 where local law sets that age for online services. If we learn that an account belongs to someone younger, we close it.

14. Cookies

The pages you are reading set no cookies at all. The application keeps your session and its settings in its own store on your machine rather than in browser cookies, and the web build uses local storage for the same purpose.

15. Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, the version and the date at the top change with it. A change that widens what we collect, or who processes it, is announced at least 30 days before it takes effect, at the email address on the account.

One thing worth saying plainly: the copy of your collections on your machine is yours. If you lose that machine, we cannot restore what was never synchronised — responses, history, secrets and files.
