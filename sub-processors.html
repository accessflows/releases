Sub-processors

Version 2026-11.1 · Effective 26 November 2026

The services that handle data on our behalf, what each one holds, and where it sits. This is the authoritative list; the Privacy Policy describes the same arrangements in the reader's words.

The list

Sub-processor What it handles Where
Supabase Accounts and sessions, the database (synchronised workspaces, shared collections, plans, verification requests), and object storage (avatars, share artwork, snapshot payloads, verification documents, shared workspaces). Japan (Tokyo)
Creem Checkout, payment, invoices and tax. Creem is the merchant of record and holds the card details; we receive the subscription, its state and the plan it carries. As its own policy states
Zoho The mailboxes the service writes to and from: no-reply@ sends account notices, and support@ and legal@ receive replies. It handles an email address, the content of a notice, and whatever a person puts in a reply. United States (Zoho Mail)
GitHub The static pages you are reading, including this one. They carry no scripts, no cookies and no analytics, and the hosting log is GitHub's. United States
Your sign-in provider (Google, GitHub or Microsoft) The sign-in itself: the identifier and email address it gives us. Nothing else. As its own policy states
Not on this list, deliberately. No analytics, crash-reporting, advertising or session-recording service is used anywhere in the product. The AI assistant is not a sub-processor: it runs on an API key the reader supplies, and the request goes from their machine to the provider they chose, not through us.

What a sub-processor is allowed to do

  • Each one handles data only to provide the part of the service described above.
  • Each is bound to confidentiality and to the data-protection terms it offers, which is where the transfer safeguards in the privacy policy come from.
  • None of them is given permission to use what it holds for its own marketing, and none of them is a place your data is sold from.

When this list changes

Adding a sub-processor, or replacing one, is announced at least 30 days before it starts handling anything, at the email address on every account. The announcement says what the new service will hold and where it sits.

If you object, you may stop using the affected part of the service, or close your account, before the date it takes effect — write to legal@accessflows.com and say so.

A transfer that has to happen at once, because a provider is failing or a security matter requires it, is announced as soon as it has happened, with the same detail.

How to keep track

This page carries a version and an effective date. Older versions can be asked for at legal@accessflows.com, and an announcement goes out whenever the list changes — you do not have to poll it.
