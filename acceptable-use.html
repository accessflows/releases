Acceptable Use Policy

Version 2026-11.1 · Effective 26 November 2026

What may not be done with an account, a share or the service. This policy is part of the Terms of Service: accepting them means accepting this.

1. Content that may not be published, stored or synchronised

  • Anything unlawful where you are, or where the person reading it is.
  • Sexual content involving minors, or material that sexualises or endangers them. We report this to the authorities and close the account; there is no warning step.
  • Malware, or material intended to harm the people who open it.
  • Material that infringes somebody's copyright, trade mark or other right, or that you have no right to publish.
  • Another person's private information — home address, identity documents, health or financial details — published without their permission.
  • Health records, payment card data, other people's government identifiers, or any other category of personal data that local law protects more strictly than ordinary personal data. The terms say the same in section 9, and the Privacy Policy says what the service synchronises.
  • Secrets, keys or tokens. A collection whose environments carry secret values is refused at publication.

2. Behaviour that is not allowed

  • Impersonating somebody, or misrepresenting which business you speak for.
  • Sharing credentials, or arranging access so that more people use the service than the accounts or seats bought — including time-sharing, or pooling one login among a team.
  • Creating or running an account by a bot or other automated means.
  • Harassing, threatening or intimidating anybody through a share, a discussion or a message.
  • Scraping or copying other people's content automatically, or using the service to build a competing directory.
  • Working around an allowance, a quota or a rate limit; probing the service for weaknesses outside our public issue tracker; or putting a load on it that it is not meant to carry.
  • Using the service from a country, or on behalf of a person, that export-control or sanctions rules prohibit.

3. What happens when this is broken

The response matches the problem, and the first step is usually a message rather than a consequence:

What happened What we do
A share that a valid notice identifies as infringing Hidden or removed, and the publisher told, so they can answer
A collection that carries secrets, or data from section 1 Unpublished until it is fixed
An allowance worked around, or an automated scrape The behaviour blocked; the account keeps running
Content that is unlawful, or that endangers somebody Removed at once, without waiting for an answer
Breaking this policy after being told, or a serious breach Community sharing withdrawn, then the account closed

A suspension because an account broke this policy ends it without a refund of the period running; a suspension for any other reason refunds the unused part. The terms say so in section 14.

4. Reporting something

A copyright or other rights complaint: legal@accessflows.com, with what the terms set out in section 8 — what the right is, the exact link, who you are, and your statement.

Anything else — abuse, a security concern, a question about this policy: support@accessflows.com, or the public issue tracker.

Working on your own API, including one that is not finished, insecure or deliberately hostile to its own testers, is the point of the application and is not a breach of this policy. This policy is about what is published to other people and what is done to the service.
