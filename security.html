Security

What we actually do, in the order that matters. This page describes the deployment as it stands — it is not a certification, and the section at the end says what we do not claim.

1. The shape of the thing

The application runs on your computer. Most of what you work on never reaches us: responses, request history, certificates and files stay in the application's own store, and the requests you send go from your machine to the endpoint you named, not through a proxy of ours.

What does reach us is the account and what you choose to synchronise or share — collections, requests, and environment values with secret-marked variables removed before they are queued.

2. Access to your data

  • Row-level rules, not application checks. A share is served only to the account that owns it and the people it was shared with; a synchronised record only to the account it belongs to; a verification document only to the desk reviewing it. The rules live in the database, so a bug in the app cannot widen them.
  • Buckets are private except where a picture is meant to be public. Verification documents and snapshot payloads are in private buckets, reached through a signed address minted for one reader. Avatars and share artwork are public on purpose — an image can then be drawn without signing anything.
  • The service role is only in the functions. It never reaches the application, and the functions that use it verify the caller's own token first.
  • Nobody here reads your workspace as a matter of course. We look at it when you ask us to, when a report of abuse or infringement needs an answer, or when the law requires it — the privacy policy says the same.

3. Secrets

A value marked secret is excluded before a synchronised record is queued, so it is not sent, stored or logged on our side. A collection whose environments carry secret values is refused at publication rather than having them stripped quietly — a published collection that is missing half its configuration is its own kind of broken.

4. Sign-in and sessions

  • Sign-in is Supabase Auth, with a password or with Google, GitHub or Microsoft. We never receive a provider password.
  • The provider flow uses PKCE and returns to a loopback address on this machine, so the code is exchanged by the application rather than by whatever browser happened to be open.
  • The session is kept in the operating system's own store for the application, not in a browser cookie, and a web build falls back to local storage.
  • An administrator role is a claim only the service role can write; a token that carries it was minted by us, not claimed by a client.

5. In transit, and at rest

  • Everything between the application and our servers is TLS; the mail path uses implicit TLS on the submission port.
  • Data at rest sits in Supabase's managed Postgres and object storage in Tokyo, under their encryption.
  • Card details never reach us: checkout, invoices and tax are Creem's, which is the merchant of record.

6. What we deliberately do not run

No analytics, no crash-reporting service, no session recording, no advertising network, and no third-party script on the pages that carry these documents. There is no telemetry in the product at all — which is a security property as much as a privacy one: nothing to leak through, and nothing to be subpoenaed from.

7. If something goes wrong

A suspected vulnerability is welcome at legal@accessflows.com, or in the public issue tracker if it is not sensitive. Tell us what you did and what happened; we will tell you what we found, and we will not pursue somebody who was trying to help.

If a breach affects your data, we tell you without undue delay — what happened, what it touched, and what we are doing — and we tell the authorities where the law requires it.

8. What we do not claim

No certification, no audit report, no contractual SLA. We hold no SOC 2, ISO 27001 or similar attestation, and this page is not a substitute for one. We do not offer a contractual uptime commitment, a contractual backup guarantee, or a right to audit. If a procurement needs any of those, say so at legal@accessflows.com and we will say plainly whether we can meet it — rather than let a document imply we can.

Your local copy is the primary one. Nothing we hold is a backup of your work, and a workspace that was never synchronised cannot be restored by us.
